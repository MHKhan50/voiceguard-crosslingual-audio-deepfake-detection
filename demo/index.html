VoiceGuard: is this voice AI-generated?

A speech deepfake detector from the VoiceGuard research project. It runs entirely in your browser: the audio never leaves your device.

Research demo, not a forensic tool. Do not use the result alone to make a decision about a person, a payment or a call.

1. Choose a voice clip

1 to 10 seconds of speech from one speaker works best. WAV, FLAC, MP3, M4A or OGG, depending on your browser.

How it works

  • The first 4 s of the clip, at 16 kHz, go through WavLM Base+ up to layer 6.
  • A small neural network trained on ASVspoof 2019 LA scores the averaged features.
  • The score is calibrated on held-out data into a log-likelihood ratio.
  • Verdict: at least 95% "Likely AI-generated", at most 5% "Likely real", otherwise "Uncertain".

How well it works

  • Equal error rate in the experiments (ASVspoof 2019 LA eval sample, 9,800 clips): 1.8% clean, 2.0% over a simulated phone line, 9.3% with added noise.
  • On {n_check} check clips, this browser model ({precision}) gave an equal error rate of {eer_demo}% (experiment pipeline: {eer_batched}%), and the same verdict as the experiment pipeline on {agree}% of clips (float32).

Limitations

  • Trained on English speech and on spoofing systems from 2019. Recent voice-cloning tools, other languages, music and strong noise can fool it.
  • Calibration fitted on clean audio is over-confident on noisy phone audio.
  • Your browser resamples the audio, which can shift scores slightly for files that are not 16 kHz.
